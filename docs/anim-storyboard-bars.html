The bar-graph version, brought up to date

The same flow that is built and live — option B, seven frames, winners first — with Act 1 drawn as bars instead of dots. Every headline, caption and badge is the shipped wording, so the only variable between this and anim-storyboard-winners-first.html is the graphic. That is what makes the two comparable if you want to test dots against bars.

Running example unchanged: Detroit Sports Legends, 1,000 votes, 5 seats, so a seat takes 200 votes (20%). Tigers 340 · Lions 260 · Red Wings 210 · Pistons 190. Three seats won outright, two on leftover votes. You voted for Amon-Ra St. Brown (Lions party).

What the bars have to carry that the dots carried for free
  • The quota is a line, not a countable group. With dots, "200 votes" is 20 dots you can see gathered. Here it is a dashed rule at every 20%, and the reader has to accept that the spacing means 200 votes.
  • Leftover votes are a stub past the last line, drawn dash-outlined — where the dots version shows a partial group that visibly fails to fill.
  • Every won seat still gets a ★, sitting under the chunk of bar that earned it. Leftover seats get a dashed-outline ★, matching the dashed ring in the dots version.
  • No scale caption. "Every dot is about 10 votes" has no bar equivalent, so that line disappears — one less thing to read, one less thing anchoring the numbers to something concrete.
Full-scale axis: each track is 0–100% of all votes, with lines at 20/40/60/80. Honest, but it means the tallest bar fills only about a third of its track — the cost of not zooming the axis.

Button weight

Three tiers on the results screen. The exit is on screen for the whole story, so while it was a solid accent button it invited you to abandon the explanation as loudly as the next frame invited you to continue it.

See how they won

Primary — solid accent. Moves you through the explanation. Also "Next" and "What it means for your vote ↓", and "Cast my vote" on the ballot. One per screen.

Show me what would happen

Secondary — outlined accent. Reveals the winner-take-all comparison. Optional, but the reader is meant to want it.

Try another election

Tertiary — quiet neutral. The exit. Full touch target, no fill, muted text, lighter weight, narrower. Present throughout without competing.

Kept as a 48px target rather than shrunk to a text link: someone who has finished and wants the next election should not have to hunt for it. The demotion is in colour and weight, not in hit area.

Frames 1, 5, 6 and 7 are identical in both. Only frames 2, 3 and 4 change.

If you test these

The question worth asking is not which looks better but whether the reader can answer "how many votes does one seat take, and did my party get there?" after seeing it once. The dots version makes the quota countable, which is the whole reason it was chosen; the bars version is calmer, shorter, and survives any turnout without changing scale. Those are real trade-offs, not a matter of polish.

The honest risk in the bars version is frame 3: it asks the reader to accept that a spacing represents 200 votes, at exactly the moment the app is trying to make the quota feel concrete.
