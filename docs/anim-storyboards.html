Rebuilding the results animation as an explanation

Storyboards, not final art. Both options use the same running example: 1,000 votes, 5 seats, so a seat costs 200 votes (20%). Gryffindor 40% · Slytherin 25% · Ravenclaw 23% · Hufflepuff 12% — four seats are won outright, and the fifth is a three-way leftover contest.

Why the current animation doesn’t teach:
  • Seat tokens appear next to the bars — nothing shows a seat being earned by 20% of the votes.
  • The “Elected” list shows only winners. The ballot order they beat is never on screen, so “even if they weren’t the top of the list” can’t land.
  • Timed beats (1.15s) rush the ideas past the reader instead of letting each one settle.
Both options below tell the story in two acts — Act 1: votes → seats per team (including the leftover seat), Act 2: inside your team, personal votes → who sits — and only zoom into the voter’s own team for Act 2 (others expandable).

Option B — “A hundred voters walk in”

The electorate becomes 100 dots (≈10 voters each — real counts stay on screen). Votes literally accumulate into seats: the strongest version of “votes add up”, at roughly twice the build.

1
The electorate
100 dots — every dot ≈ 10 voters. Your own dot is marked “you”.
2
Voters gather
Each dot walks to its team. Gryffindor draws 40 of the 100.
3
Benches become seats
A bench holds 20 dots (200 votes) — every full bench elects one seat: 2–1–1–0. That’s 4 seats. One is still empty, and three teams are left holding part-benches.
4
The leftover seat
1 seat still empty · a full bench = 20
The part-benches get compared where they stand — no reshuffling. Hufflepuff’s 12 voters sit closest to a full bench — closer than Slytherin’s 5 or Ravenclaw’s 3 — so Hufflepuff takes the empty seat.

My recommendation: Option B, tap-to-advance. It’s the only version where “votes add up to seats” is literally what your eyes watch happen, and the leftover seat — the hardest rule to explain in words — becomes trivial: the fullest unfinished bench wins it. Option A is the right call if you want it shipped faster; its Act 2 is identical, and A can later be upgraded to B without redoing the math or copy.

Pacing: in both, beats should advance on tap (“Next”), not on a timer — this is an explanation, and readers absorb at different speeds. Auto-play stays as a fallback after ~6s idle so the screen never feels stuck.

And yes — alphabetize the ballot

Your instinct is right, and it makes Act 2 land harder.

Today the rosters are listed in fame order, so the winner is usually the top name and the “not the top of the list” moment almost never shows up. Alphabetical by first name makes the printed order visibly arbitrary — when Ron (5th) beats Harry (1st), the lesson tells itself. Implementation note: sort at display time only; the fame model behind the simulated electorate stays attached to the right people. (Fun alternative from real elections: Tasmania rotates candidate order per ballot — “Robson rotation” — but it makes screenshots inconsistent and Act 2 harder to narrate.)
