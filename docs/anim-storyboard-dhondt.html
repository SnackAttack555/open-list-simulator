Option B, tabulated with D’Hondt

Same election as the main storyboard — 1,000 votes, 5 seats, Gryffindor 40% · Slytherin 25% · Ravenclaw 23% · Hufflepuff 12% — but the seats are awarded by D’Hondt, the world’s most-used method (Spain, Portugal, most of Latin America, EU elections).

How D’Hondt works: seats are handed out one at a time. A team’s bid for the next seat is its votes ÷ (seats it already holds + 1) — in dot terms, its voters must split into equal groups, one per seat, and the team whose groups stay biggest wins. There’s no bench price and no leftovers: winning a seat halves (then thirds…) your next bid, and that shrinking does the balancing work.

Each seat goes to the biggest bid. Gryffindor’s 400 takes seat ① — and winning halves its next bid to 200. That’s why Slytherin (250) and Ravenclaw (230) take seats ② and ③ before Gryffindor can bid again.
4
Bidding again means splitting
⚡ Gryffindor1 200 each — wins ④
🐍 Slytherin2 125 each
🦅 Ravenclaw3 115 each
🦡 Hufflepuff 120
A team’s voters must now split evenly across its seats-plus-one. Gryffindor’s two groups of 200 are still the biggest bid on the board — bigger than Hufflepuff’s whole, undivided 120. Seat ④: Gryffindor.
5
The last seat
⚡ Gryffindor14 133⅓ — wins ⑤
🐍 Slytherin2 125
🦅 Ravenclaw3 115
🦡 Hufflepuff 120 — never biggest
Even split three ways, Gryffindor’s groups (133⅓ votes each) still edge Hufflepuff’s 120. Seat ⑤: Gryffindor. Final: 3–1–1–0 — Hufflepuff never holds the biggest bid, so it never seats anyone.

The two methods disagree about exactly one seat. Largest remainder asks “whose leftover is biggest?” (Hufflepuff’s 120). D’Hondt asks “whose per-seat groups stay biggest?” (Gryffindor’s 133⅓). Both are real, widely used rules.

What this means for the simulator: D’Hondt slightly favors bigger teams, and at 5 seats that bites — the 12% team goes from seated to shut out. It also costs the one-sentence rule: there is no “20% of the vote wins a spot” under D’Hondt, and the bench metaphor stops working (no fixed bench size). My advice stands: build the app on largest remainder, whose story fits one sentence and one picture — and keep this page as the “what if we used the world’s most common method?” companion. A method toggle could be a v2 teaching tool.
