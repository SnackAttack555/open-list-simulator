Option B — the chosen flow

Act 1 frames are drawn with the app's real geometry: one dot = 10 votes, groups of 20 dots, same rows, rings and badges as the shipped component. Running example: Detroit Sports Legends, 1,000 votes, 5 seats, so a seat takes 200 votes (20%) and the grid holds 100 dots. Tigers 340 · Lions 260 · Red Wings 210 · Pistons 190. Three seats are won outright and two go to the biggest leftovers. You voted for Amon-Ra St. Brown (Lions party).

Decisions folded in since the last version
  • B is the flow. A is kept below only as a record of what is currently built; C is rejected.
  • The old B2 is gone — the opening grid of loose dots. The dots now first appear already sorted into parties.
  • Every won seat gets a ★, earned or leftover. The dashed ring is what still marks a leftover group; "+" is gone from both the badges and the text.
  • Each Act 1 frame has one text block, above the dots. No caption underneath — the reader's eye no longer crosses the drawing mid-sentence.
  • The opening winners frame shows vote counts.
Renumbering: dropping the old B2 shifts everything up by one. The frames you called B6 (who fills the seat) and B7 (your vote) are now B5 and B6. Panels below carry both the number and a name so this doesn't bite again.

Right now B5 always shows your party. If your party won no seats there is nothing to show inside it, so B5 switches to the party that won the most seats. That leaves two questions: why the reader is suddenly looking at a party they didn't vote for, and what B6 says when the honest answer is "your vote elected nobody."

Two versions below. Both are drafts — nothing is built yet. They differ only in whether B6 makes the argument or just states the fact.
